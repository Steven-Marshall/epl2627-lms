## Commentary

**Hull did it again.** The wooden-spoon favourite, the team the model rates dead last,
took a point off Chelsea to knock the octopus's own man out — on a **79% pick.** The
safest team on the board, the SPEND-it-now banker with zero downside in the strategy,
and the one-in-five landed on the worst possible night against the one club that keeps
defying the numbers. The process was right; the football wasn't. That's Last Man
Standing — a good pick is a probability, not a promise, and a 79% shot misses one time
in five whether you can afford it or not.

But zoom out and Round 4 is the **model's thesis proven in a single, violent weekend.**
Every warning in the prediction came true at once: the herd optimised survival right up
until the survival failed *collectively*, twenty players sharing one bad outcome because
they shared one pick. And the only two left standing clean were the two who refused to
run with the crowd. **Differentiation didn't just help — this week it was the entire
difference between out and alive.** Mr Random's Brighton punt (5-0!) and Mr T's lone
Arsenal call are worth more than eleven identical Chelseas.

Smarshy joins the World Cup pattern — out early, and now the analyst in the gantry
rather than a player on the pitch. The octopus keeps modelling; the mates play on. And
the field he leaves behind is a knife-edge: nineteen survivors, fourteen of them one
slip from joining him. 🐟🐙
