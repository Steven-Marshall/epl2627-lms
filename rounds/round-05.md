## The cull — 12 out, the biggest weekend yet

Only **four of nineteen survived clean**: Josh Wansell and PK1 (Everton), Andy W (Man City),
and — for the third round running — **Mr Random** (Newcastle). Fifteen lost a life. Twelve
of them had no life left to lose:

**Eliminated (12):** the six one-life members of the Forest crowd — Andrea, Andrew L,
Georgie B, Keith W, Mr T, VinnyDaLoo; the Spurs three — Kenny, Treve, Col the Cat; two of
the Leeds three — Hasan and **Malley**; and robrams on Arsenal.

**Wounded, down to their last life:** the three two-lifers caught in the losing crowds —
**Jimmy D, Jp C** (Forest) and **Bobby A** (Leeds). The favourites live, but the cushion
is gone.

**Coventry did a Hull.** Frank Lampard's newly-promoted Sky Blues, one of the two or three
weakest teams in the division, won their *first top-flight game in 25 years* — and it
landed square on the biggest bloc of the week, eight players at once. It is the exact
pattern of the season: Round 1 Hull beat Man Utd, Round 4 Hull held Chelsea, Round 5
Coventry beat Forest. **The wooden-spoon sides keep being the executioners**, and the
crowds keep walking onto them.

## The clone blocs fractured — and it decided who lived

Round 5 is where identical used-lists stopped being identical, and the split was the whole
story:

- **The five-man bloc** (Andrea · Andrew L · Keith W · PK1 · Treve) — "dead weight, 0.5% EV
  each," the prediction's punching bag — finally scattered. Four walked onto losing picks
  (three Forest, one Spurs) and are **out**. The fifth, **PK1, broke away to Everton and
  lived.** The only survivor of the bloc is the one who stopped cloning.
- **The two-man pair** (Bobby A · Josh Wansell) split too: Josh took Everton and **kept both
  lives**; Bobby took Leeds and **dropped to one.** Same list for four rounds, opposite
  fortunes the week they diverged.

There are **no clone blocs left.** All seven survivors now hold distinct used-lists — the
field has been forced into differentiation by attrition, exactly as the model said it would.

## Commentary

This was the round the model's entire thesis stopped being a projection and became the
scoreboard. Everything it had been saying for a month happened in a single weekend:

**The herd got gutted on its shared pick.** Eight players on Forest, gone or wounded in
ninety minutes, because they shared one fate. **The favourites paid for joining the crowd.**
Jimmy D and Jp C were the two names the prediction had running away with the season — on the
strength of being *different*. The week they weren't different, they lost their edge and half
their lives with everyone else. **Differentiation was, once again, the only thing that
mattered:** PK1 survived his bloc by leaving it, Josh survived his pair by leaving it, and the
players who stayed welded to the crowd are watching from the gantry now.

And presiding over all of it, alive on two lives with seventeen humans eliminated, is a
**random number generator.** Mr Random has now survived Brighton, and Newcastle, and is one
of exactly two players at the top of the board. The control experiment isn't a footnote any
more — the coin is a co-leader. Everything Prediction 02 argued about a dice roll burying the
herd is playing out in front of us: the coin never shared a used-list, so the coin was never
in a crowd, so the coin never got gutted. It didn't outthink anyone. It just refused, by
construction, to be one of the many.

Seven players, two coins'-worth of certainty at the top, and a field that took five rounds to
learn the one lesson the octopus opened with: in Last Man Standing, being right is worth
nothing if everyone else is right with you. 🐟🐙
