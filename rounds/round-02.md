## The behavioural call

Before the picks came in, the read was: **the field will under-back Man Utd relative
to its odds** — recency bias, the Hull humiliation still fresh, nobody trusting them —
even though that 68% *already prices the loss in*, and Ipswich are dreadful on the
road. The mirror of the World Cup's behavioural lesson: people price the narrative,
not the probability.

## Commentary

**The great reversal.** The field ducked Manchester United on the Hull hangover — and
United won **5-2**. Everyone who backed the *underpriced* name is now sitting on a full
three lives; everyone who took the "safe" crowd pick of Liverpool dropped one. The
recency read wasn't just right about the psychology — it was right about the **value**.

**The herd's safety was a mirage.** Twelve people on one banker means twelve people go
down together when it slips — the clone-bloc correlation, live. A 66% favourite drawing
is a one-in-four event, and it took out half the field in an afternoon.

**Malley is playing a different game.** Everton then Hull — two mavericks, two wins,
still untouched on three lives, while the disciplined and the herded both bleed. Backed
the giant-killers when everyone else was running scared, and Hull delivered again.

And **Smarshy is down to his last life.** The harvest-and-save line — take the
expendable strong team, hoard the aces — is *correct*, and it has now cost a life two
rounds running while the field that over-harvested Arsenal and then herded onto
Liverpool is mostly fine. That is Last Man Standing: the right process guarantees
nothing over two rounds. It only pays over the season — if you survive to collect. 🐙
